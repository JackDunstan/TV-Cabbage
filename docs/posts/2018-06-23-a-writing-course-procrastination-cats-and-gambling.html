A writing course, procrastination, cats and gambling.

2018-06-23T11:17:00.001+01:00https://www.tvcabbage.co.uk/2018/06/a-writing-course-procrastination-cats.html

A
writing course, procrastination, cats and gambling.

What a great time of the day to get up and start
working. 6am downstairs, computer on, kettle on. One thousand words by 9am. Ambitious I know,
but there’s nothing like setting oneself an unrealistic target to prophesise
the inevitability of one’s own failure, and to assist wholeheartedly in that
failure, which, when examined closely, is actually a motivation.

procrastination in action

5am dawn chorus. They are so happy it’s a new day and
they’ve survived the night. I feel the same way. First, attend to earthly
matters. Ablutions. Back to bed, listen to birds tweeting, discern patterns,
identify bird, estimate distance away, guess which ones are nesting in the tree
outside. Isn't it great to be alive? I lie there wishing I could find out more
out about birds tweeting, and identify them.

I used to have said to me “use the dictionary”, now
they’ve got the whole knowledge of mankind at their fingertips on Google. “No,
I can't be bothered”. The whole class becomes one of philosophical argument and
persuasion as to illustrating the advantages of an education and how it may
assist one in surviving the rigours of Western industrialized capitalist
society. I even dream when awake, about my job.

Still, new challenges. I check my oil filled right
eye’s vertical and horizontal ability by looking at the light shining through
the crack in the curtain. 22° tilt down from right to left. Maybe better
tomorrow.

I reach over and turn on Radio Four just in time for
‘Tweet of the Day’. This is the time of day where I start timing things,
probably because its ‘Farming Today’ which I don't listen to, because I
actually find it interesting, but don't want to explore that avenue just yet.

The big T begins to arouse by now from her deep
slumber. Oh good, I can stop stroking this ginger cat that keeps attacking me
and sometimes sleeps on the bed between us like a big old lump of a big belly.
He's not quite ‘wheel around in the wheelbarrow’ size yet, but he's working on
it. The big T goes off to have a shower. If it’s an 'I'm washing my hair' day I
add 10 minutes on to the downstairs schedule. Downstairs, kettle on, back door
open, computer on, draw curtains, collect last night detritus from the table and
clear it up [usually empty crisp packets, cake boxes, sweet wrappers, empty
tubs of cream, pizza boxes, hash fudge, TV dinner and black plastic detritus –
don't worry it will break down into a billion tiny plastic pieces.

I ready the Big T’s Jordan's sugar-free muesli,
quartered strawberries, a couple of grapes, a little bit of banana topped with
organic semi-skimmed milk and a dab of full fat Greek yogurt. I have mine and
it’s naked. Plenty of time for the sugar craving later. When I hear her finishing
up upstairs put the kettle on, again, have the TV news on in the background but
not too loud.

I note a few jobs to do, and most importantly, think of
what the next thing I’m going to eat is. We have the big T off by 7.20 so it’s
time to work.

I see the John Oliver show is recorded from last night.
Get in. I'll just have a quick look at that to keep abreast of current
late-night show political satirists obsessions. Then I'll do some writing. It
feels good just to spew out the flow to only myself as a rough first draft that
no one will hear. I think to myself I really need to go to a class that could
dispense such writing gems and tips. I need more of them.

Well, it's 10 o'clock and time for a cup of tea. Did I
get 1000 words out? No I got 795 words out. I watched John Oliver show then I
watched Westword, not Westworld, but word; a fan show introduced by Lauren Laverne.
Time to further explore the realm of procrastination. Now, I was going to make dinner
but the Big T suggested she buy a pizza and we put some extra cheese and, I
thought, what a genius, more time to focus on being creative. Oh, hang on, the
cats need feeding. I'll do that just as soon as I bought this lottery ticket.

Prepared dinner for two, consumed. Red wine swirling
it’s time for a couple of hours of quality time with the love of one’s life –
television– then to bed at 10 for ‘story time’. I think we are on book 5 (book
2 of The Tawny Man) of Fitz and The
Fool. A series of multiple trilogies read to a partially sighted ex teacher by
a sonorously voiced woman. It’s the only book I have read to me by a real
person in real time and it’s very special.

My original idea was to get 50 columns of a thousand
words of #TOSH in the bank as it were, and get some
publication/Website/magazine/if all else fails my own blog/don’t give up just
yet.

I arrived 6.31, one minute late, which was conducive to
one feeling ill at ease for being late, as I am a fraud and not really a writer
at all and I really wanting to improve my discipline. Rick greeted me warmly on
the doorstep. I have a complex relationship with this man and want to explore
that a little more and I’m really looking forward to getting to know him and my
fellow genii.

Rick steers the conversation round to books we’ve read
and or reading and I feel embarrassed that now I appear to be looking at loads
of different books at the same time but it’s true; I have Google read out books
to me while I’m on my computer, I can listen to an audiobook and I still do
have a Kindle, which when I get my sight back I’ve still got a stack to read on
there. But to me, it’s the sheer pleasure of just reading that gets me and my
mind jumping around. So if want a crime thriller or I want a nonfiction on the
nature of time or the cosmos that’s what I’m interested in that’s what I read.
Don’t forget to have an old, classic sci-fi on the go.

Rick’s cat is called Olive and is very fluffy and
friendly. A bit like him.

My Lottery ticket cost £2.
